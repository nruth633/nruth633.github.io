A permanent version of the breadboard

Nearly every microcontroller exercise needs the same handful of things: something to turn, something to press, some LEDs to prove the code reached the line you thought it did, and a display for anything more informative than blinking. Rebuilding that on a breadboard each time is slow, and the failures it produces are wiring failures rather than interesting ones.

This shield makes that set permanent — pots, buttons, status and RGB LEDs, and an OLED, all on a board that seats on an Uno.

Selectable-voltage expansion

The part worth pointing at is the expansion headers. Breaking out I²C and SPI is routine; letting you choose the voltage on those headers is what makes the board keep earning its place.

The Uno runs at 5 V, but a large share of interesting peripherals — most modern sensors, most displays — are 3.3 V parts, and feeding them 5 V is a good way to destroy them. Making the rail selectable means a 3.3 V sensor can be attached directly without a level shifter improvised on the spot, and it removes an entire class of mistake that only announces itself as a dead component.

Related work

This is one of three Uno-format boards — the others being the IMU shield and the DemoSat balloon payload, which also uses the shield form factor.
