DemoSat High-Altitude Balloon Payload

  • KiCad
  • 120 g
  • $115
  • 259 mW
  • Design only

Background

This started from an existing balloon payload, and the task was to redesign it. When I checked whether the original could still be built, two of its four sensors were out of production. You can't buy the humidity or pressure parts anymore, so the original design can't be built again, whether or not it worked before.

So most of the project was picking replacement parts while staying under the mass, cost and power limits.

Constraints

Requirement Budget Design figure
Mass under 200 g ~120 g
Cost under $147 ~$115
Power less than the original (~90 mA) 259 mW typical, 459 mW with all LEDs on

Sensor selection

Each replacement had to be better than the part it replaced, not just available. I compared resolution, accuracy and power draw, and looked for parts that run at 3.3 V so the whole board could share one supply voltage.

Measures Original Chosen Why
Acceleration ADXL335 ADXL345 I²C instead of analog, 13-bit, range to ±16 g rather than ±3 g, ~140 µA at 3.3 V
Pressure HSCDANN015PAAA5 DPS310 300–1200 hPa, runs on 1.7–3.6 V instead of 5 V, and only ~1.7 µA at 3.3 V
Humidity HIH-4030 HIH-9120 I²C, ±1.7 % accuracy against ±3.5 %, physically much smaller
Temperature TMP36 DS18B20 Digital rather than analog, 9–12 bit instead of ADC-limited to 10, ±0.5 °C against ±2 °C, waterproof package suits external mounting

Three of the four use I²C, so they share one two-wire bus. The DS18B20 is 1-Wire instead. It uses an extra pin, but it comes as a sealed probe that can sit outside the enclosure and measure the outside air instead of the inside of a warm box.

Using digital sensors also gets around the Arduino's 10-bit ADC, which limited the accuracy of the original design no matter how good its analog sensors were.

The layout is a 2-layer board, 69.5 × 54.0 mm (2.74 × 2.13 in). OSH Park quoted $29.05 for three.
