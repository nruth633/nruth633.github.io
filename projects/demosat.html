DemoSat High-Altitude Balloon Payload

  • KiCad
  • Arduino Uno shield
  • I²C
  • SD logging

What it is

A sensor payload flown on a high-altitude balloon, built as an Arduino Uno shield and designed and soldered from scratch in KiCad. Four sensors log the flight environment to an SD card for recovery and analysis after landing.

What separates this from a bench project is that it only gets one attempt. Once the balloon is released there is no debugging, no reflashing, and no reseating a loose connector — so component selection has to account for where the payload is going, not just whether the circuit works on a desk.

Sensor suite

Part Measures
DS18B20 Temperature
HIH9120 Relative humidity
DPS310 Barometric pressure — the altitude proxy
ADXL345 3-axis acceleration — ascent, burst, and descent dynamics

Designing for the environment

Components were selected against the flight profile rather than room conditions. A high-altitude balloon climbs through air that gets down toward −50 °C and pressure that falls to a small percentage of sea level, so parts have to hold their specified accuracy at temperatures well below the commercial range most datasheets lead with, and pressure sensing has to stay useful at the low end rather than saturating.

Logging to SD rather than transmitting means the data survives loss of radio contact — the payload keeps recording regardless of whether anything is listening.
