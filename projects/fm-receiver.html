What it is

A working FM broadcast receiver built from discrete components — no receiver IC doing the hard part. Signal is picked up, tuned, recovered, and amplified through stages I chose and assembled individually.

Tuning the band

The front end is an LC tank: a hand-wound 0.15 µH coil paired with a 1–30 pF variable capacitor. Sweeping that capacitor moves the resonant frequency across the full 88–108 MHz broadcast band, which is what turns the tuning knob into station selection.

Winding the inductor by hand is the interesting part. At these frequencies a fraction of a microhenry decides where the band lands, and the value depends on turn count, coil diameter, and spacing — so the coil gets adjusted physically until the tuning range covers the stations you actually want.

Recovery and audio

An S9018 — a small-signal transistor with enough gain-bandwidth to be useful at VHF — handles the RF stage and signal recovery. From there the audio drops to an LM386, a classic low-power audio amplifier, which drives a speaker directly.
