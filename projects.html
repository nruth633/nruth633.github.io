Projects

Things I've designed, built, and debugged

Coursework and personal projects — things I built because I wanted to know whether they'd work. Several have public design files on GitHub.

  • Electromagnetic Rail Launcher

    ~330 J stored in a 13,200 µF bank charged to 225 V from an 11.1 V LiPo, with inrush held to 85 mA by twelve series resistors chosen for wattage, not just resistance.

    • 330 J
    • Power electronics
    • High voltage

  • Measuring the Silicon Bandgap

    Pulled the silicon bandgap (1.31 eV) and Boltzmann's constant (1.56 × 10⁻²³ J/K) out of one transistor with a 9 V battery and a multimeter, then combined them into a flat voltage reference.

    • Semiconductor physics
    • Measurement
    • R² = 0.97

  • DemoSat Balloon Payload

    Rebuilt a high-altitude balloon payload after two of its four sensors went out of production. Landed at 120 g and $115 against a 200 g / $147 budget.

    • KiCad
    • Sensors
    • I²C

  • Transistor Analog Synthesizer

    An all-analog instrument with no microcontroller — astable oscillator, light and voltage pitch control, slow modulator, LM386 output. Every stage verified on the scope.

    • Discrete analog
    • Audio
    • LM386

  • FM Radio Receiver

    A discrete receiver on breadboard — hand-wound coil, S9018 RF stage, LM386 into a speaker. It pulled in one station and not much else, and the write-up is mostly about why.

    • RF
    • Analog
    • Discrete

  • Electromagnetic Pulse Demonstrator

    A capacitor-discharge EMP generator: a 9 V high-voltage step-up converter charges a 20 kV ceramic capacitor bank, discharged through a hand-wound coil to produce a pulsed magnetic field.

    • High voltage
    • 20 kV
    • Magnetics

  • Battery Tester Board

    Eight LM324 comparators against a resistor ladder, lighting an eight-LED bar for battery voltage measured under a 20 Ω load. Schematic complete; it ran out of term before layout.

    • KiCad
    • LM324
    • Schematic only

  • Arduino Uno I/O Shield

    An Uno-format board that gives a 5 V microcontroller an analog voice: a 10-bit R-2R ladder DAC buffered into a 3.5 mm output jack, plus an MPU6050, an SSD1306 OLED, pots and buttons.

    • R-2R DAC
    • KiCad
    • MPU6050

  • 3-Wheel Balancing Robot

    Drives from a phone browser over the ESP8266's own WiFi, with live IMU telemetry, on a control board I designed and hand-soldered down to the 64-pin QFP. The balance loop is the part that was never finished.

    • STM32

    One slice of the DU IEEE chapter's LED cube: three daisy-chained 74HCT595 shift registers driving 24 transistor cathode sinks, so eight RGB columns run off a five-wire bus.
